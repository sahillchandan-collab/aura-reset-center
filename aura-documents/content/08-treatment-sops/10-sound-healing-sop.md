# Sound Healing Sessions — Treatment SOP

> Document No. AUR-TRT-10 · Version 0.1 (Draft) · Owner: General Manager · Effective: [Date]

> The instrument list, session length, group size and volume limits for Sound Healing are not yet confirmed. Screen with AUR-FRM-03 section 10 and the universal checks, together with section 4 of this SOP.

## 1. Purpose

Sound Healing is a facilitator-led session, offered 1:1 or in a small group. The guest rests while a facilitator plays instruments such as singing bowls and gongs [confirm instruments]. Slow, layered sound invites the nervous system to settle and supports the energy-healing side of a guest's programme. It is a wellness practice, not a treatment for any medical or hearing condition. It is often the closing step of a visit.

## 2. Who may deliver

- A sound healing facilitator approved by the General Manager. This may be a Healer or a Host with sound healing training [training / qualification — confirm].
- Group sessions: one facilitator for up to [__] guests.
- The facilitator holds current first aid [certificate ref.].
- If the facilitator is one of the two Hosts on shift, the other Host covers the floor. Do not schedule Host-guided modalities that need the facilitator during the session (see AUR-TRT-11).

## 4. Contraindications summary

Check the universal checks in AUR-FRM-03 and ask about the following.

| Category | Item | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Medical clearance | Epilepsy or seizure history | Clearance before first session; quieter, steadier sound; no sudden loud strikes |
| Adapt | Sound sensitivity, hyperacusis, tinnitus, misophonia or migraine | Lower volume; no gong or a soft gong only; place guest furthest from instruments; ear plugs offered |
| Adapt | Pregnancy | No instruments placed on the body; gentle volume; guest's doctor's advice if in doubt |
| Adapt | Hearing aids or cochlear implants | Guest chooses to keep in, turn down or remove; warn of possible feedback whistle |
| Adapt | Pacemaker or other implanted device | No on-body instruments; guest's doctor's advice if in doubt |
| Adapt | PTSD, trauma or anxiety | Explain sounds first; avoid sudden loud strikes; guest may keep eyes open and sit up |
| Do not proceed | Acute illness, fever or feeling very unwell today | Rebook |

> Refer questions to the Senior Host on shift, then the General Manager, who may consult the [Medical Director named on the licence].

## 6. Guest preparation

1. Ask about hearing, sound sensitivity, epilepsy, pregnancy and implanted devices.
1. Explain the instruments and that some sounds will be deep and felt in the body.
1. Agree whether any bowl may be placed on or near the body (1:1 only, with consent).
1. Make clear the guest may sit up, open their eyes, use ear plugs or leave at any time.
1. Show the quiet signal: a raised hand.

## 7. Step-by-step delivery

1. Welcome and settle. Begin with the Aura ritual.
   - *"Let your body find the mat. In for four, out for six."*
1. Set expectations.
   - *"You may hear deep tones, and feel some of them in your body. If anything is too much, raise your hand and I'll soften it."*
1. Opening, quiet sound only, about [___] min.
   - *"There's nothing to do. Let the sound move around you."*
1. Main journey, about [___] min. Build volume slowly. Never strike a gong loudly without a gentle build.
1. Watch guests throughout. Respond at once to any signal by softening or pausing.
1. Close with fading sound, then silence, about [___] min.
   - *"Rest in the quiet. Notice what remains."*
1. Return slowly. Lights up gradually.
   - *"Begin to move gently. Roll onto your side when you're ready. Take all the time you need."*

## 8. Monitoring and stop criteria

Stop or soften the sound, and check on the guest, if they:

- signal, ask to stop, cover their ears or appear distressed
- report pain in the ears, ringing that grows, headache, dizziness or nausea
- show any sign of a seizure or unusual movement
- become strongly emotional and wish to sit up or leave

If a seizure occurs, stop all sound at once and follow section 12. Note every reaction on the session log.

## 9. Post-session care, refreshments and suggested combinations

- Guests sit quietly for a few minutes before standing.
- Offer water or herbal tea [menu to confirm].
- Ask one gentle question: *"How does your body feel now?"* With consent, share notes with the guest's Healer.

Suggested combinations (see AUR-TRT-11):

- Any sequence → Sound Healing, as the closing step of a visit.
- Dry Float Bed → Sound Healing, for sleep.
- Guided Breathwork → Sound Healing, for calm.

## 10. Cleaning and reset

Follow AUR-SAF-02.

1. Bag used towels, blanket covers and eye-pillow covers for the in-house laundry.
1. Wipe mats and bolsters with the approved surface product [product — to be confirmed].
1. Wipe any bowl that touched a guest's body or clothing, as advised by the instrument maker [care instructions — to be confirmed]. Dry fully.
1. Store instruments safely; secure gongs on their frames.
1. Sign the room off.

## 11. If equipment faults

If an instrument cracks, a stand or frame becomes unstable, or lighting or air-conditioning fails, stop that instrument and move guests clear if needed. Continue with other instruments only if safe, or close the session calmly. Report under the Equipment Fault & Out-of-Service Procedure (AUR-SOP-09) and tell the Senior Host. A falling gong is a serious injury risk. Never use an unstable frame.

## 12. Emergency actions

Follow AUR-SAF-04.

- Seizure: stop all sound, protect the head, move objects and instruments away, do not restrain, time the seizure; call 998 and follow section 4.11.
- Faint or unwell: section 4.1.
- Injury from a falling instrument: section 4.6; call 998 for any head injury.
- Fire alarm: section 4.7. Guide guests out calmly; count everyone out.
- In a group, the facilitator stays with the person affected. The second Host calls 998 and cares for the rest of the group.

Complete an Incident Report Form (AUR-SAF-04, section 7) for every incident or near miss.
